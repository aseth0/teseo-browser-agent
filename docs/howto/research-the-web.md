# Research with web search

Ask something the open page cannot answer: the chat searches the web, reads the best results without touching your
tabs, and answers with its sources.

**Example:** you plan a visit to the (invented) Harbor Museum and want this week's opening hours and the adult ticket
price.

## What you need

- A model set up. Nothing else: the browser's own search engine is used unless you pick another in
  **Settings → Web search → Search engine**.

## What you type

> Find this week's opening hours and the adult ticket price of the Harbor Museum, and link your sources.

## What you see

1. The chat shows each search and each page it reads.
2. The pages open in background tabs: muted, folded in a tab group named *Agent*, and closed when read. Your own tabs
   stay as they are.
3. The answer comes with links. To see a page yourself, ask *"open the museum's page"*: it opens in a new tab.

## What you approve

- **Searches** run without asking. Tick **Ask before each search** in **Settings → Web search** to see each query
  first.
- **Reading a page** follows that site's policy. The first time on a site you allow reading it; on a site set to
  **Ask before acting**, a card shows *Read … in a background tab*. **Allow on *site* ▾ → In this conversation** stops
  the cards for that site.
- **A page that wants you signed in** shows a card *site asks you to sign in*: **Show the tab**, sign in there, then
  **Done, read it**. Or **Skip** it.

## If it goes wrong

- **A captcha or a block page**: the chat says so. Try again later, or choose another **Search engine**.
- **Outdated or conflicting answers**: ask it to compare its sources and give the date of each.
- **A long page cut short**: ask it to *"read the rest of that page"*.
- **Searches in your history**: they run in your browser like your own, so its history keeps them.
